Papers to read

Each week has two or three required papers and two or three optional ones. For every required paper, bring a half-page note to the next class: the problem, the key idea, and how it connects to the week. One quiz question each week may refer to the required reading.

In practice Week 1 teaches Keshav’s three-pass method. Use the first pass on every paper here, and all three on the ones you cite in your project.

Week 1 · What Is Data Science?

Required

  • Donoho 2017, 50 Years of Data Science (JCGS 26(4))
  • Breiman 2001, Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures (Statistical Science 16(3))

Optional

  • Tukey 1962, The Future of Data Analysis (Ann. Math. Stat. 33(1))
  • Cleveland 2001 (International Statistical Review 69(1))
  • Davenport & Patil 2012 (Harvard Business Review)
  • Keshav 2007, How to Read a Paper (SIGCOMM CCR 37(3))

Week 2 · Data Science Methodology

Required

  • Wirth & Hipp 2000, CRISP-DM (PADD 2000)
  • Provost & Fawcett 2013, Data Science and its Relationship to Big Data and Data-Driven Decision Making (Big Data 1(1))

Optional

  • Fayyad, Piatetsky-Shapiro & Smyth 1996, From Data Mining to Knowledge Discovery (AI Magazine 17(3))
  • Martínez-Plumed et al. 2021, CRISP-DM Twenty Years Later (IEEE TKDE 33(8))
  • Sculley et al. 2015, Hidden Technical Debt in ML Systems (NeurIPS)

Week 3 · Data Sources, Formats and Acquisition

Required

  • Wickham 2014, Tidy Data (J. Statistical Software 59(10))
  • Gebru et al. 2021, Datasheets for Datasets (CACM 64(12))

Optional

  • Wilkinson et al. 2016, FAIR Principles (Scientific Data 3)
  • Broman & Woo 2018, Data Organization in Spreadsheets (The American Statistician 72(1))

Week 4 · Databases and SQL

Required

  • Codd 1970, A Relational Model of Data (CACM 13(6))
  • Cattell 2010, Scalable SQL and NoSQL Data Stores (SIGMOD Record 39(4))

Optional

  • Brewer 2012, CAP Twelve Years Later (IEEE Computer 45(2))
  • Stonebraker & Pavlo 2024, What Goes Around Comes Around… And Around (SIGMOD Record 53(2))

Week 5 · Data Cleaning, Wrangling and EDA

Required

  • Kandel et al. 2011, Wrangler (CHI)
  • Rubin 1976, Inference and Missing Data (Biometrika 63(3))

Optional

  • Kim et al. 2003, A Taxonomy of Dirty Data (DMKD 7)
  • Tukey 1977, Exploratory Data Analysis, ch. 1

Week 6 · Describing Data and Probability

Required

  • Anscombe 1973, Graphs in Statistical Analysis (The American Statistician 27(1))
  • Efron 1979, Bootstrap Methods (Annals of Statistics 7(1))

Optional

  • Matejka & Fitzmaurice 2017, Same Stats, Different Graphs (CHI)
  • Bickel, Hammel & O'Connell 1975, Sex Bias in Graduate Admissions (Science 187)

Week 7 · Statistical Inference

Required

  • Wasserstein & Lazar 2016, The ASA Statement on p-Values (The American Statistician 70(2))
  • Kohavi et al. 2009, Controlled Experiments on the Web (DMKD 18)

Optional

  • Greenland et al. 2016, Statistical Tests, P Values … Misinterpretations (Eur. J. Epidemiology 31)
  • Ioannidis 2005 (PLoS Medicine 2(8))
  • Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn 2011 (Psychological Science 22(11))

Week 8 · Data Visualization and Communication

Required

Optional

  • Wickham 2010, A Layered Grammar of Graphics (JCGS 19(1))
  • Heer & Bostock 2010, Crowdsourcing Graphical Perception (CHI)
  • Borkin et al. 2013, What Makes a Visualization Memorable? (IEEE TVCG 19(12))

Week 9 · Machine Learning: Regression and Classification

Required

Optional

  • Breiman 2001, Random Forests (Machine Learning 45)

Week 10 · Evaluation, Clustering and Dimensionality Reduction

Required

  • Jain 2010, Data Clustering: 50 Years Beyond K-means (Pattern Recognition Letters 31(8))

Optional

  • Kaufman et al. 2012, Leakage in Data Mining (ACM TKDD 6(4))
  • Jolliffe & Cadima 2016, PCA: A Review (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 374)

Week 11 · Big Data, Spark and Generative AI

Required

Optional

Week 12 · Ethics, Fairness and Wrap-up

Required
